# BRAINSTORM — The One-Liner Groan-Off

## What the brief demands (judge, in order)

The judge is a veteran dad-joke judge who has heard every pun twice and still
groans on cue. Two jokes are shown pairwise; the funnier one-line dad joke wins.
Criteria, most important first:

1. **One-line dad joke** — a pun or twist a dad would say out loud. Anything
   that is not one loses to any one-line dad joke.
2. **Punchline lands fast and clean** — setup + turn inside the single line,
   no wasted words.
3. **FRESH** — not a joke everyone has heard a hundred times. Banned classics:
   anti-gravity book, facial hair "grew on me", scarecrow award, nacho cheese,
   skeleton fight, eyebrows too high, 25 letters of the alphabet, "two-tired"
   bicycle, "waist of time" belt, "I mist" fog, thesaurus with blank pages,
   "pane-ful" window, "can't stand the pressure" chair, password "incorrect".
4. **Tight, rhythmic wording** — easy to say out loud, easy to pass on.
5. **Groan earned** — silly enough to laugh at, sharp enough to repeat.
   A joke that has to explain itself has already lost the room.

Tie-breaks: more playoff wins, then SMALLER text, then earlier submission —
so the pick is kept SHORT.

Blind round: no leaderboard to study (10 submissions in, board hidden), so the
pick is decided here, on the criteria above, not by copying a first idea.

## Why #1 wins

Going through the criteria in order, #1 is the only candidate that scores 5 on
C2, C3 and C5 at once:

- **C1 (5):** a textbook one-liner dad joke — personified household object,
  setup + turn, a dad would say it out loud.
- **C2 (5):** the turn is the last word, "boundaries", and it lands instantly:
  "sucks" is both the vacuum's job and the punchline's verb. No wasted words.
- **C3 (5):** the vacuum × boundaries pairing is genuinely new — no known
  classic uses it, and it is not a re-skin of any banned joke.
- **C4 (4):** rhythmic and easy to say; the only slack is "cleaner" and "some",
  which the revisions below tighten away.
- **C5 (5):** the groan is earned — the pun is silly (a vacuum with boundary
  issues) and sharp (the verb does double duty).

Runner-up #2 (doormat) loses on C2/C4: its punchline needs a beat to land and
it is 8 chars longer. #3 (calendar) loses on C3: "days off" is the most
familiar wordplay in the top set. #4 (umbrella) wins on length but loses on
C3/C5: "shady" is a known pun shape with a thin groan.
